Noticing My Yang Habits in Yin

In a seated forward fold (Caterpillar), I would almost automatically approach it as Paschimottanasana: inhale to lengthen, exhale to go deeper, then use muscular effort to hold that position for three to five minutes.

I had changed the duration, but not the way I was practising.

Austin described repeated lifting and folding with the breath as a yang method: engaging the muscles to move deeper. His instruction for Yin was to find the edge, stay there and relax.

His explanation helped me recognise the effort I was carrying into the pose. He described how muscular contraction pulls things tight, and how relaxing allows things to lengthen. I was still pulling myself forward and holding on.

Austin described Yin as placing more emphasis on connective tissues, including fascia, tendons and ligaments. He also stressed that these cannot be isolated from muscles. The distinction is about focus.

In Bananasana, he made the approach tangible: stop where the body naturally stops, avoid pulling further, and give it time. If the position becomes easier, a small adjustment may become available.

I'm used to making an adjustment to deepen a stretch. I'm less used to waiting and noticing whether the experience changes before I do anything more.

Another instruction stayed with me: “You can't force yourself to relax.” It made me wonder whether I might bring that same habit of effort to softening — turning relaxation into another task to accomplish.

What I'm learning is to find a position where I can begin to let go, and give myself time to notice what happens.

Three to five minutes gives me time to notice those habits. It doesn't automatically change them.
