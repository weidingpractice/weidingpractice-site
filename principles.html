练习原则What holds across poses

体式页记的是一个体式里发生了什么；这里记的是在很多体式里都成立的规律。Each pose page records what happens in that pose. This page records what holds across many of them.

注意力点要成对。Attention points come in pairs.

要做好 alignment，就把注意力放在成对的两个点上。左右成对：两条腿，屁股两边，肩胛骨两边，手两边。前后也成对：劳宫放在骶骨上，和前面的肚脐成对，呼吸时前后就对上了。只放一个点，身体还是找不到 alignment。

To find alignment, rest your attention on points that come in pairs. Left and right: two legs, two sides of the buttocks, two shoulder blades, two hands. Front and back too: rest Laogong on the sacrum and pair it with the navel, and as you breathe, front and back line up. With one point, the body still can't find its alignment.

幅度永远不是重点。Range is never the point.

转得深还是浅，跟身体条件、练习时间长短有关。我们练的永远不是幅度，而是觉知和呼吸的深度。

How deep you go depends on your body and how long you have practised. You never practise range; you practise awareness and the depth of the breath.

transition 要转臀，转才不卡。Turn the hips in transitions.

臀部是可以转的，而且一定要转。大多数人的 transition 不转臀部，这是个很大的问题。两个提示：高弓步到战士二；战士二到 Skandasana。

The hips can turn, and they must. Most people keep the hips still in transitions, and that is a big problem. Two cues: High Lunge to Warrior II; Warrior II to Skandasana.

站立力线：什么时候用哪个点。Standing lines: which point, when.

  • 整条站立力线：涌泉 → 太溪 → 血海 → 环跳
    The whole standing line: Yongquan → Taixi → Xuehai → Huantiao
  • 用后腿的力：涌泉对上太溪
    Using the back leg: Yongquan lines up with Taixi
  • 转臀的 transition：高弓步 → 战士二，战士二 → Skandasana：臀部正中心最深的点（意象）
    Hip-turning transitions: High Lunge → Warrior II, Warrior II → Skandasana: The deepest point in the centre of each buttock (an image)
  • 高弓步 → 战士三，高弓步 → 站立扭转：环跳
    High Lunge → Warrior III, High Lunge → standing twist: Huantiao
  • 站立后弯（站立下轮）：涌泉 → 太溪 → 血海 → 腹股沟前面（冲门或气冲，待确认）
    Standing backbend (drop-back to Wheel): Yongquan → Taixi → Xuehai → front of the groin (Chongmen or Qichong, to confirm)

这里记录的是练习时注意力放在哪里，不是诊断，也不是治疗。有伤病或疼痛，请先咨询医生。

These notes are about where attention goes in practice. They are not diagnosis or treatment. If you have an injury or pain, see a doctor first.
